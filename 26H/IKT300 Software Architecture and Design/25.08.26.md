### Coupling and Cohesion
#### Coupling 
- an indication of how much two components dependent on each other to function properly. 
Coupling is inevitable, but we need to make sure we know what is coupled, and want to reduce it. 

#### Cohesion
- an indiciation of how well to modules fit together. Usually achieved by assigning a single functionality to components. 
Putting related objects together. And avoiding putting unrelated stuff together.

We want Low coupling and high cohesion.

#### Process "Rules of Thumb"
- The architecture should be the product of a single architect or a small group of architects with an identified technical leader.
	- This approach gives the arcitecture its conceptual integrity and technical consistency.
	- This recommendation holds for agile and open source projects as well as "traditional" ones.
- There should be a strong connection between the architect(s) and development team.
- The arrchitecture should base the architecture on a proioritized list of well specified quality attrigbute requirements.
- The architecture should be documented using views. THe views should address the concerns of the most important stakeholders in support of the project timeline. 
- The architecture should be evaluated for its ability to deliver the systmems important quality attributes
	- This should occur early in the life cycle and reapated throughout. 
- The architecture should lend itself to incremental implementation
	- Create a "skelatal" system in which the communication paths are exercised but which at first ha minimal funcitonality.
- The architecture should feature well-defined modules, whose functional responsibilities are assigned on th eprinciples of information hdigin and seperation of concerns.
	- The information-hiding modules should encapsulate things likely to change.
	- Each module should have a well defined interface that encaspulates or "hides" the changeable aspects from other software.
- Unless your requirements are unprecedented your quality attributes should be achieved using well-known architectural patterns and tactics specific to each attribute. 
- The architecture should never depend on a particular version of a commercial product or tool. If it must, it should be structured so that changing to a different version is straightforward and inexpensive. 
- Modules that produce data should be seperate from modules that consume data. 
	- This tends to increase modifiability
	- Changes are frequently confined to either the production or the consumption side of data.
- Don't expect a one-to-one correspondence between modules and components. 
- Every process should be written so that its assignment to a specific processor can be easily changed, perhaps even at runtime.
